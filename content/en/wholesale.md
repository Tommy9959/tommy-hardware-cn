## How wholesale ordering works

1. **Choose the models and quantities** — send a list, or ask for the best sellers in each category for the Nigerian market.
2. **Get a proforma invoice** with FOB or CIF prices, carton dimensions and gross weight, so freight can be calculated before you commit.
3. **Pay 30% deposit** by T/T; production starts the same week.
4. **Production 15-25 days** — 20-30 days for custom finishes or OEM packaging.
5. **Balance 70% before shipment**, then loading and documents (invoice, packing list, bill of lading, plus SONCAP/Form M/PAAR support).
6. **Sea freight 25-35 days to Lagos** (Apapa or Tin Can) or 30-40 days to Onne, then inland trucking.

## What makes a wholesale order work

- **Mix models, not just categories.** A first container that carries 5-10 fast-moving SKUs sells through faster than one filled with a single design.
- **Watch the weight, not only the volume.** Hinges and pipes are heavy; padlocks and handles are light. Balancing the two inside one container is what keeps the freight per piece low.
- **Order packaging that survives the port.** Cartons sized for 20 sets of locks or 20-24 pieces per box reduce damage and make counting at the warehouse faster.
- **Settle the documentation before the deposit.** SONCAP needs the product details up front; discovering it after production is what causes demurrage.

## Who this suits — and who it does not

The wholesale model fits importers, distributors and project buyers who resell in cartons or supply construction sites. It does not fit a buyer who needs one lock for a house: there is no local stock, no single-unit sale and no installation service. For retail, a Lagos or Onitsha hardware shop is the right channel.
