### Tip 2: Match the Lock to Your City's Climate

**Lagos / Port Harcourt / Calabar (coastal humidity):**
- Buy iron mortise locks with anti-rust coating or electroplating
- If possible, go for stainless steel cylinders (brass cylinder with stainless pins is good too)
- For padlocks: stainless steel body > iron body in coastal areas

**Abuja / Kano / Jos (dry inland):**
- Standard iron mortise locks with powder coating work fine
- SS201 components are more than enough here
- Budget options sell well — rust isn't a big issue

**Quick story:** A Port Harcourt customer bought standard iron padlocks from us in 2020. 8 months later they were covered in rust. He blamed the product. I told him: "Brother, you're in Port Harcourt. Salt air kills iron locks. Let me send you stainless steel padlocks." We swapped the order. The stainless steel ones? Still going strong 4 years later.

### Tip 3: Check the Finish

This is the #1 thing new importers miss. Two iron door locks can look identical in photos. But one has a thin coat of paint (budget) and the other has proper powder coating or electroplating.

**Quick test:** Scratch the finish on the back with a coin. If it flakes off easily, that lock will rust quickly in Nigeria's climate. A good finish won't scratch off.

### Tip 4: Don't Go for the Absolute Cheapest Price

**Red flags to watch for:**
- Price 20%+ below the market average
- Supplier can't show you factory photos or business license
- No Nigerian customer references
- Wants 100% payment upfront
- Doesn't know what SONCAP is

If your supplier doesn't know SONCAP, run. We handle SONCAP for all our Nigerian customers — free of charge.

## Section 5: Iron Door Lock vs Other Materials — What Should You Stock?

Many new importers ask me: "Should I stock iron door locks or stainless steel locks?"

**The short answer: Stock iron door locks for volume, stainless steel for specific markets.**

### When to Sell Iron Door Locks

- **Price-sensitive markets** — Most residential buildings in Nigeria use iron locks. They cost less and work well for standard doors.
- **Interior doors** — Humidity isn't as bad indoors. Iron locks with good coating last 8-12 years inside.
- **Budget customers** — If your market is contractors building low-cost housing, iron locks at $2.50-$3.50 are exactly what they need.

### When to Recommend Stainless Steel or Brass

- **Coastal cities** — Any door exposed to salt air needs stainless steel or brass
- **Premium properties** — Luxury estates, government buildings
- **Exterior gates** — Where weather exposure is constant

**My recommendation for new importers:** Start with 80% iron door locks in the $3.00-$4.50 range. Add 15% stainless steel padlocks for coastal clients. Keep 5% for samples and special orders.
