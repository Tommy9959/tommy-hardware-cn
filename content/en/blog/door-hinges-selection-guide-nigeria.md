If you're building or renovating in Nigeria—whether it's a residential compound in Lekki, a commercial building in Ikeja, or a school project in Abuja—door hinges are one of those small components that can make or break the whole installation.

After supplying door hardware to the Nigerian market for over a decade, here's our field-tested guide on which hinges work where, and which ones cause problems.

## 1. Butt Hinges — The Reliable Workhorse

**Best for:** Wooden doors (both interior and exterior)

Butt hinges are the most common hinge type in Nigerian construction. They're simple, strong, and inexpensive. Available in stainless steel (304 preferred for coastal areas) and mild steel with various finishes.

**What to look for:**
- **Material:** SS304 for Lagos/coastal areas (salt air corrodes mild steel fast). For inland cities like Abuja, galvanized steel is acceptable.
- **Size:** 4"×3" (100×75mm) and 5"×4" (125×100mm) cover 90% of residential needs. Commercial doors need larger sizes.
- **Pin type:** Loose pin for easy door removal. Non-removable pin for external doors (tamper-proof).

**Our HH-001 and HH-004 series** are the most popular choices for Nigerian resellers. They offer the right balance of strength and price point.

## 2. Pivot Hinges — For Heavy Doors

**Best for:** Heavy wooden doors, metal doors, gate applications

Pivot hinges carry the door weight differently than butt hinges—they distribute weight to the floor and header rather than the door frame. This makes them ideal for heavier doors common in Nigerian commercial buildings.

**Key consideration:** Pivot hinges require precise installation. Make sure your contractor has experience with them. A poorly installed pivot hinge will cause door sag within months.

## 3. Self-Closing / Spring Hinges

**Best for:** Kitchen doors, commercial kitchens, fire doors

Nigeria's bustling restaurant scene and commercial kitchens need self-closing hinges. They automatically close doors, preventing smoke and fire spread.

**Important:** Spring tension must be adjustable. Nigerian temperatures cause metal fatigue faster—non-adjustable springs lose tension within 6-12 months.

## 4. Concealed (Invisible) Hinges

**Best for:** Modern interior design, premium homes

Growing trend among Nigerian architects and interior designers. Concealed hinges hide inside the door and frame for a clean, modern look.

**Caveat:** They require precise routing during installation. Replacement parts are harder to find locally in Nigeria. We recommend stocking extra hinges if using this type.

## Material Selection Guide — Climate Matters

Nigeria has diverse climates, and your hinge choice should reflect it:

| Region | Climate Challenge | Recommended Material |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| Lagos / Coastal | High humidity, salt air | SS304 stainless steel |
| Port Harcourt | Rainforest, high corrosion | SS304 stainless steel |
| Abuja / North | Dry, dusty | Galvanized steel or SS304 |
| Enugu / East | Moderate humidity | SS304 or brass |

**One rule:** Never use mild steel hinges on external doors in coastal areas. We've seen replacements needed within 3 months.

## Common Problems We See in Nigeria

### Problem 1: Wrong screw selection
Nigerian contractors often use low-quality screws that strip easily. Use stainless steel screws matching the hinge material. This small detail prevents 50% of hinge problems.

### Problem 2: Under-sized hinges for heavy doors
A standard 3.5" hinge cannot carry a 50kg iron door. Always check the hinge weight rating against your door weight. When in doubt, go one size up.

### Problem 3: Hinge corrosion from cleaning chemicals
Many Nigerian cleaning products contain bleach or strong acids. These corrode hinge finishes fast. Wipe hinges with a dry cloth—don't spray cleaner directly on them.
