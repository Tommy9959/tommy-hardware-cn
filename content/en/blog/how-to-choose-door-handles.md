## Understanding Door Handle Types

### 1. Lever Handles

**Best for:** Interior doors, exterior doors, commercial buildings

**Pros:**
- Easy to use (great for children and elderly)
- Modern appearance
- ADA compliant
- Wide variety of styles

**Cons:**
- Can catch clothing
- May be less secure than knobs

**Price Range:** $0.85 - $5.50 per piece

### 2. Door Knobs

**Best for:** Traditional homes, bedroom doors, bathroom doors

**Pros:**
- Classic appearance
- Generally more secure
- Less likely to catch clothing
- Cost-effective

**Cons:**
- Harder to use for some people
- Limited design options

**Price Range:** $1.20 - $6.00 per piece

### 3. Pull Handles

**Best for:** Sliding doors, cabinet doors, commercial entrances

**Pros:**
- Sleek, modern look
- Easy to clean
- Durable
- Good for heavy doors

**Cons:**
- Requires more hand strength
- Limited to specific door types

**Price Range:** $2.50 - $15.00 per piece

### 4. Smart Door Handles

**Best for:** Modern homes, offices, high-security areas

**Pros:**
- Keyless entry
- Remote access control
- Activity monitoring
- Modern technology

**Cons:**
- Higher cost
- Requires power source
- Technical maintenance

**Price Range:** $50.00 - $300.00 per piece

## Size and Measurement

### Backset Measurement

The backset is the distance from the edge of the door to the center of the handle hole.

**Common Backsets:**
- 60mm (most common for interior doors)
- 70mm (common for exterior doors)

**How to Measure:**
1. Measure from the edge of the door to the center of the existing hole
2. If no hole exists, measure from the edge to where you want the handle

### Handle Length

**Standard Lengths:**
- Interior doors: 120-150mm
- Exterior doors: 150-180mm
- Commercial/Heavy doors: 180-250mm

### Rose/Backplate Size

**Common Sizes:**
- Small: 50mm diameter
- Standard: 55-60mm diameter
- Large: 65-70mm diameter

**Tip:** Larger roses cover more wear marks on older doors.

## Installation Tips

### Tools Needed

- Drill and drill bits
- Screwdriver (Phillips and flathead)
- Tape measure
- Pencil
- Chisel
- Hammer
- Hole saw (usually 50mm or 54mm)
- Spade bit (usually 25mm)

### Step-by-Step Installation

1. **Mark the Position**
   - Measure and mark the handle height (usually 900-1000mm from floor)
   - Mark the backset distance

2. **Drill the Main Hole**
   - Use a hole saw to drill through the door
   - Keep the drill perpendicular to the door

3. **Drill the Latch Hole**
   - Use a spade bit to drill from the edge
   - Meet the main hole

4. **Install the Latch**
   - Insert the latch mechanism
   - Secure with screws

5. **Install the Handles**
   - Insert one handle through the door
   - Align with the other handle
   - Secure with screws

6. **Test the Handle**
   - Ensure smooth operation
   - Check that the latch retracts properly
   - Adjust if necessary

### Common Installation Mistakes to Avoid

❌ **Wrong backset measurement**  
✅ Measure twice before drilling

❌ **Drilling at an angle**  
✅ Use a drill guide or take your time

❌ **Hole too large**  
✅ Use the correct size hole saw

❌ **Forgetting to lubricate**  
✅ Apply graphite lubricant to the latch

❌ **Over-tightening screws**  
✅ Snug is enough, don't strip the screws

## Maintenance Guide

### Daily Care

- Wipe with a soft, damp cloth
- Dry immediately to prevent water spots
- Avoid harsh chemicals

### Weekly Cleaning

- Use mild soap and water
- For stainless steel: wipe with grain
- For brass: use brass cleaner if needed

### Monthly Maintenance

- Check and tighten loose screws
- Lubricate moving parts with graphite lubricant
- Inspect for wear or damage

### Annual Deep Cleaning

- Remove handles and clean thoroughly
- Check latch mechanism
- Replace worn parts if needed

### Finish-Specific Care

**Stainless Steel:**
- Use stainless steel cleaner
- Wipe with grain direction
- Avoid chlorine-based cleaners

**Brass:**
- Polish every 3-6 months
- Use brass-specific polish
- Consider lacquered brass for low maintenance

**Bronze:**
- Dust regularly
- Occasional waxing for protection
- Embrace the patina development

**Nickel:**
- Mild soap and water
- Avoid abrasive cleaners
- Dry immediately after cleaning

## Climate Considerations

### Coastal Areas (Nigeria, Dubai, etc.)

**Recommended:**
- ✅ Stainless Steel (SS304 or SS316)
- ✅ Aluminum Alloy
- ✅ Brass

**Avoid:**
- ❌ Iron-based materials
- ❌ Unprotected zinc alloy

**Why:** Salt air accelerates corrosion

### Dry Climates

**Recommended:**
- ✅ All materials suitable
- ✅ Zinc alloy (cost-effective)
- ✅ Iron + aluminum (budget option)

**Considerations:**
- UV resistance for finishes
- Dust protection for mechanisms

### Humid Climates

**Recommended:**
- ✅ Stainless Steel
- ✅ Aluminum Alloy
- ✅ Brass with protective coating

**Avoid:**
- ❌ Unprotected iron
- ❌ Low-quality finishes

**Why:** High humidity promotes corrosion

## Security Considerations

### Exterior Door Handles

**Must-Have Features:**
- Anti-snap cylinders
- Reinforced strike plates
- Minimum 30mm throw latch
- Secure fixing screws (inside)

**Recommended Materials:**
- Stainless Steel
- Solid Brass
- Heavy-duty Zinc Alloy

### Interior Door Handles

**Standard Features:**
- Privacy function for bathrooms/bedrooms
- Passage function for living areas
- Dummy handles for closets

**Security Level:** Lower priority than exterior

### Commercial Applications

**Requirements:**
- Grade 1 or Grade 2 security rating
- Fire-rated if required
- ADA compliant
- High-cycle testing (500,000+ cycles)

## FAQs

### Q: How long do door handles last?

**A:** Quality door handles should last:
- **Stainless Steel:** 15-25 years
- **Brass:** 20-30 years (with maintenance)
- **Zinc Alloy:** 10-15 years
- **Iron + Aluminum:** 8-12 years

### Q: Can I mix different finishes in my home?

**A:** Yes! Modern design allows mixing finishes. Just ensure they complement each other. Popular combinations:
- Brushed nickel + matte black
- Polished chrome + brushed brass
- Oil rubbed bronze + satin nickel

### Q: Are expensive door handles worth it?

**A:** For high-traffic areas and exterior doors, yes. Premium handles offer:
- Better durability
- Smoother operation
- Better security
- Longer warranty
- Better appearance retention

### Q: How do I know if a door handle is good quality?

**A:** Check for:
- Weight (heavier usually means better quality)
- Finish quality (no bubbles, even coating)
- Smooth operation
- Solid construction (no rattling)
- Warranty offered
- Certifications (ISO, CE, etc.)

### Q: Can I replace door handles myself?

**A:** Yes! Most door handles are DIY-friendly if:
- You have basic tools
- The new handle fits existing holes
- You follow installation instructions
- You take your time and measure carefully
