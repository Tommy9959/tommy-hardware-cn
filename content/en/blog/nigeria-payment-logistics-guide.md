## Payment Methods: What's Safe in 2026?

### The Big 5 Payment Methods

| Method | Safety | Cost | Speed | Best For |
|--------|--------|------|-------|----------|
| **T/T (Telegraphic Transfer)** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Low | Fast | Repeat suppliers, trusted relationships |
| **L/C (Letter of Credit)** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ | High | Slow | Large orders ($50k+), first-time suppliers |
| **RMB Settlement** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Low | Fast | Suppliers accepting RMB, cost savings |
| **Western Union/MoneyGram** | ⭐⭐ | High | Fast | Small sample orders only |
| **Escrow Services** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Medium | Medium | High-value first orders |

---

### 1. T/T (Telegraphic Transfer) — Most Common

**How it works:**
- 30% deposit before production
- 70% balance before shipping OR against copy of B/L

**Pros:**
- Low fees ($20-$50 per transfer)
- Fast (1-3 days)
- Simple process

**Cons:**
- Risk if supplier disappears (mitigate with verified suppliers)
- No buyer protection after deposit

**Best Practices:**
- Only use with **verified suppliers** (Alibaba Gold, ISO certified, known brands like SOLA)
- Never send 100% upfront
- Ask for company bank account (not personal)
- Get bank details in writing on company letterhead

**SOLA's T/T Terms:**
- 30% deposit, 70% against B/L copy for first orders
- 30% deposit, 70% at 30 days for established customers

---

### 2. L/C (Letter of Credit) — Safest for Large Orders

**How it works:**
- Your bank issues L/C to supplier's bank
- Supplier ships goods and presents documents
- Bank releases payment only after verifying documents

**Pros:**
- Highest security for both parties
- Bank guarantee
- Good for first-time large orders

**Cons:**
- High fees ($500-$2000 per L/C)
- Complex documentation
- Slow (5-10 days to process)

**Best For:**
- Orders over $50,000
- First-time suppliers
- Government contracts

**Banks in Nigeria Issuing L/Cs:**
- Zenith Bank
- GTBank
- Access Bank
- First Bank
- UBA

---

### 3. RMB Settlement — Growing in Popularity

**How it works:**
- Pay supplier directly in Chinese Yuan (RMB/CNY)
- Uses Cross-Border RMB Settlement system

**Pros:**
- Better exchange rates (avoid USD conversion)
- Lower fees
- Faster processing
- Some suppliers offer better pricing for RMB

**Cons:**
- Requires RMB account in Nigeria or Hong Kong
- Not all suppliers accept RMB

**How to Set Up:**
1. Open a domiciliary account that handles RMB (Zenith, GTBank)
2. Or use a Hong Kong intermediary account
3. Confirm with supplier they accept RMB

**SOLA's RMB Policy:**
- ✅ Accepts RMB settlement
- 2% discount for RMB payments
- Provides RMB pricing upon request

---

### 4. Escrow Services — Good for High-Risk Situations

**Recommended Escrow Providers:**
- Alibaba Trade Assurance (best for Alibaba orders)
- Escrow.com (independent)
- Your bank's escrow service

**How it works:**
1. You send payment to escrow
2. Escrow confirms to supplier
3. Supplier ships goods
4. You confirm receipt
5. Escrow releases payment to supplier

**Best For:**
- First-time orders with new suppliers
- High-value orders ($10k-$50k)
- When you want extra security

---

### 5. Western Union/MoneyGram — Samples Only

**Never use for bulk orders!**
- No buyer protection
- Sent to individuals (not companies)
- High risk of fraud

**Only use for:**
- Sample orders under $500
- Small urgent payments

## Shipping Options: LCL vs FCL vs Air Freight

### The 3 Main Shipping Methods

| Method | Cost | Speed | Best For |
|--------|------|-------|----------|
| **LCL (Less than Container Load)** | Medium | 35-45 days | Small orders (<10 CBM) |
| **FCL (Full Container Load)** | Low | 30-40 days | Large orders (>20 CBM) |
| **Air Freight** | High | 5-10 days | Urgent orders, samples |

---

### 1. LCL (Less than Container Load)

**What it is:**
- Your cargo shares a container with other shipments
- Charged by volume (CBM) or weight

**Cost Estimate (China → Lagos):**
- Door handles/locks: $80-$120 per CBM
- Minimum charge: 1-2 CBM ($150-$300)

**Transit Time:**
- Ningbo/Shanghai → Lagos: 35-45 days
- Door-to-door: add 7-14 days

**Best For:**
- First-time test orders
- Small restocks (<10 CBM)
- Mixed product orders

---

### 2. FCL (Full Container Load) — Most Cost-Effective

**Container Sizes:**
- **20GP:** ~28-30 CBM, 28 tons max
- **40GP:** ~58-60 CBM, 28 tons max
- **40HQ:** ~68-70 CBM, 28 tons max

**Cost Estimate (China → Lagos):**
- 20GP: $2,500-$4,500 (all-in)
- 40GP: $4,000-$6,500 (all-in)
- 40HQ: $4,500-$7,000 (all-in)

**Transit Time:**
- Ningbo/Shanghai → Lagos: 30-40 days
- Apapa Port clearance: 7-14 days

**What You Can Fit in a 20GP:**
- Door handles: ~50,000 pieces
- Door locks: ~30,000 pieces
- Hinges: ~100,000 pairs
- Mixed container: ~$30,000-$60,000 worth of hardware

**Best For:**
- Regular importers
- Orders over $30,000
- Planning to hold inventory

---

### 3. Air Freight — For Urgent Orders

**Cost Estimate (China → Lagos):**
- $5-$10 per kg (depending on volume/urgency)
- Minimum charge: ~$200-$300

**Transit Time:**
- Airport-to-airport: 3-5 days
- Door-to-door: 7-10 days

**Best For:**
- Urgent sample orders
- Small high-value items
- Restocking fast-selling items

---

### Key Ports in Nigeria

**1. Lagos (Apapa & Tincan Island) — 90% of Imports**
- Main port for hardware
- Good infrastructure but congestion common
- Expect delays during peak season (Nov-Dec, Mar-Apr)

**2. Port Harcourt (Onne Port) — Oil & Gas Hub**
- Closer to Eastern Nigeria
- Less congestion than Lagos
- Good for Port Harcourt, Aba, Enugu area

**3. Calabar — Southern Gateway**
- Newer port
- Less congestion
- Good for Cross River, Akwa Ibom

## Customs Clearance & Documentation

### Required Documents (Complete List)

**From Your Supplier (SOLA will provide all these):**
1. Commercial Invoice
2. Packing List
3. Bill of Lading (B/L)
4. Certificate of Origin (CO)
5. SONCAP Certificate (see below)
6. Test Reports (if required)
7. Insurance Certificate

**You Need to Prepare in Nigeria:**
1. Form M (Mandatory!)
2. PAAR (Pre-Arrival Assessment Report)
3. SONCAP Certificate
4. Customs duty payment

---

### Step 1: Get Form M

**What is Form M?**
- Mandatory document for all imports into Nigeria
- Shows what you're importing, value, supplier
- Must be obtained BEFORE shipping!

**How to Get Form M:**
1. Go to your bank (with Form M department)
2. Provide: Proforma Invoice, Supplier details, Product specs
3. Bank processes through CBN (Central Bank of Nigeria)
4. Get Form M number (valid for 6 months)

**Pro Tip:**
- Get Form M BEFORE production starts
- If you wait, you'll have storage fees in China
- SOLA can help you prepare Form M documentation

---

### Step 2: Get PAAR

**What is PAAR?**
- Pre-Arrival Assessment Report
- Nigeria Customs uses it to calculate duties
- Generated from Form M and shipping documents

**How to Get PAAR:**
1. Submit shipping documents to your bank
2. Bank forwards to Nigeria Customs
3. Customs issues PAAR with duty assessment
4. You pay duties based on PAAR

---

### Step 3: Pay Customs Duties

**How Duties are Calculated:**
- Based on CIF value (Cost + Insurance + Freight)
- Duty rates for door hardware: ~20-35%
- VAT: 7.5% additional
- Add port/handling fees (~5-10% extra)

**Example Calculation:**
```
Product value (FOB): $20,000
Shipping & insurance:  $3,000
CIF value:           $23,000

Duty (25%):          $5,750
VAT (7.5%):          $1,725
Port fees:             $800
TOTAL DUTIES:       $8,275
```

**How to Pay:**
- Through your bank (most common)
- Or at customs cash desk (riskier)
- Some clearing agents offer credit (for trusted clients)

---

### Step 4: Clear Customs

**Options:**
1. **Do it yourself** — Complex, not recommended for first-timers
2. **Use a clearing agent** — Recommended, costs ~2-5% of cargo value

**Choosing a Clearing Agent:**
- Ask for references from other importers
- Get 2-3 quotes
- Make sure they understand hardware imports
- Ask about their success rate with your product type

**Red Flags:**
- Promises "zero duty" or "under-the-table" deals
- Asks for upfront payment before seeing documents
- Cannot provide references

## SONCAP Certification: Step-by-Step

### What is SONCAP?

**SONCAP** = Standard Organization of Nigeria Conformity Assessment Program

- Mandatory for most hardware products
- Proves products meet Nigerian standards
- Without it, your cargo gets seized!

---

## Avoiding Scams & Common Pitfalls

### Top 5 Scams to Watch For

**1. Fake Supplier Scams**
- They copy real supplier websites
- Ask for payment to personal accounts
- Disappear after receiving deposit

**How to Avoid:**
- Verify company registration (ask for business license)
- Check Alibaba Gold Supplier status
- Never send to personal accounts
- Request factory video call
- Order small sample first

---

**2. "Low Price" Scams**
- Prices 30-50% below market
- They take your deposit, send nothing or garbage

**How to Avoid:**
- If it's too good to be true, it is
- Get 3 quotes from different suppliers
- Compare specs carefully (not just price)
- Ask for samples first

---

**3. Fake Documents Scams**
- Fake SONCAP certificates
- Fake Bill of Lading
- Fake inspection reports

**How to Avoid:**
- Verify SONCAP on SON website
- Confirm B/L with shipping line
- Use third-party inspection (if needed)
- Work with established suppliers like SOLA

---

**4. "Customs Assistance" Scams**
- "My brother works in customs, can get it cleared cheap"
- Ask for upfront payment, then disappear

**How to Avoid:**
- Use registered clearing agents only
- Get references
- Never pay cash upfront
- Get everything in writing

---

**5. Currency Scams**
- "Send USD to this Dubai account, better rate"
- Then they disappear

**How to Avoid:**
- Use official banking channels only
- Never send to third-party accounts without written confirmation
- Confirm bank details directly with supplier (via call/email)

---

### Common Logistics Mistakes to Avoid

**1. Not Getting Form M Before Shipping**
- Result: Storage fees in China, delays
- SOLUTION: Get Form M BEFORE placing order

**2. Under-Valuing Goods on Invoice**
- Result: Customs fines, delays, cargo seizure
- SOLUTION: Declare accurate value

**3. Wrong Product Description**
- Result: Customs questions, delays
- SOLUTION: Use exact product names from Form M

**4. Not Insuring Cargo**
- Result: Total loss if ship sinks or cargo damaged
- SOLUTION: Always get marine insurance (SOLA can arrange)

**5. Poor Packing**
- Result: Damaged goods, higher losses
- SOLUTION: Ask SOLA for Nigeria-specific packing (extra protection!)
