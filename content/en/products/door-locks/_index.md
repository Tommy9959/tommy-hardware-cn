## Door lock types in Nigeria — and which one we supply

Three types of lock get sold in Nigerian building-material markets, and buyers mix them up more often than they should:

- **Iron plate mortise lock with an aluminium handle** — the volume product. The lock body sits inside the door edge and the plate carries the handle and cylinder. It is what steel security doors, shop doors and most residential timber doors are prepared for, and it is what traders in Onitsha Main Market, Ogidi and Alaba restock week after week.
- **Cylindrical (knob) lock** — a bored-through lock for light interior doors. Cheaper to install, weaker against forced entry. Interior bedrooms only.
- **Rim lock** — surface-mounted on the door face. Still common on older buildings and gates because the door needs almost no preparation.

We manufacture and export the **first type only**. That focus is deliberate: one format, 57 models across the 755, 837, 955, 905 and 9129 series, so a trader can keep the same door preparation across an entire container and still offer customers a choice of handle design.

If you need cylindrical or rim locks, say so before ordering — we will tell you honestly that we are not the right factory rather than substitute a different format into your PI.

## Where the $2.50-$5.00 comes from

The range is not cosmetic. Four things move the unit price from one model to the next:

1. **Plate thickness and gauge.** A heavier steel plate resists twisting when a door slams and keeps the handle from working loose after a year. Thin plate locks are what fail first on site.
2. **Cylinder quality.** The pin stack and the brass content of the plug decide how long the key keeps turning cleanly in Harmattan dust. This is the single largest cost difference between a $2.50 lock and a $4.50 lock.
3. **Handle material and finish.** Aluminium handles with AB antique bronze plating (the default) cost less than solid or zinc-alloy handles with two-layer plating.
4. **Finish and carton spec.** BN/SN (brushed or satin nickel) and MAE (matt antique english) finishes are produced on request and change the unit price — ask for a quote per finish, not a single blended price.

That is why we quote **per model and per finish**, and why the price printed for a 755 series lock is not automatically the price for a 905.

## What a steel security door in Nigeria actually needs

Nigerian steel doors are fabricated locally to a fairly standard cut-out: one plate footprint, one handle position, one key side. Because the local fabricators have settled on that format, the safest buying rule is to **stay inside the 755 / 837 / 955 / 905 / 9129 series**. A trader who switches to an exotic plate size ends up with stock that fits nothing on the street.

Two practical points we repeat to every new buyer:

- **Keep one key side.** Left-hand and right-hand versions of the same model must be ordered in the ratio your customers actually buy — usually close to 50/50, but ask your biggest two or three accounts before you fix the ratio in the PI.
- **Cartons are 20-24 pcs.** At 19.8-26.1 kg per carton, a mixed order of 5-10 models still stacks cleanly on a pallet, which matters when your clearing agent is moving the consignment through Apapa or Tin Can.

## Onitsha, Ogidi, Aba, Alaba, Ladipo — who buys what

The Nigerian door lock market is not one market:

- **Onitsha Main Market and Ogidi (Greater Onitsha)** — the densest hardware cluster in the South-East and the one that feeds most inland traders. Volume buyers here test one or two models, then repeat in multiples of 400.
- **Aba (Ariaria)** — price-sensitive, and a strong market for the basic $2.50-$3.50 band.
- **Lagos — Alaba and Ladipo** — the most spec-conscious buyers. They compare finish quality and cylinder feel before they compare price, and they resell on to retail shops across the South-West.
- **Port Harcourt and Onne** — coastal humidity makes finish quality a selling point; the same lock sells better when it does not spot within a season.

If you tell us which market you supply, we will tell you which two or three models your competitors are most likely already stocking.

## Humidity, Harmattan and finish choice

The default **AB antique bronze** finish is electroplated, and its life depends on the thickness of that plating. Coastal buyers — Lagos, Port Harcourt, Warri — should not accept a plating below the 8 µm range, because salt air will bubble it within one to two seasons. Inland and dry-season markets (Kano, Maiduguri, Jos) can take the standard plating and save the money.

The same logic covers Harmattan dust: it grinds inside an unlubricated cylinder. A lock that is graphite-powdered once a season outlives one that is never touched. We cover the repair side in detail in our [door lock jamming fix guide](/blog/door-lock-problems-nigeria-fix-guide/).
