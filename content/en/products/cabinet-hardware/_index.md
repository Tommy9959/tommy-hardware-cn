### Hole spacing: the specification that decides reorders

A pull handle is only sellable if the hole centres match the drill jig the workshop already uses. Get this wrong and the whole carton comes back.

- **32 mm and 64 mm** — small drawer pulls, bathroom vanity units, older kitchen carcasses.
- **96 mm** — the single most common centre in Nigerian kitchen and wardrobe work.
- **128 mm** — standard on wider kitchen drawers and modern wardrobe doors.
- **160 mm and above** — large appliance-style pulls, commercial joinery, hotel furniture.

We drill to ±0.3 mm and pack screws sized for 16-18 mm board. If your customer base is mostly replacement work, stock the sizes that already exist on the door rather than the newest catalogue shape.

### Materials and finishes that hold up

- **Zinc alloy (CH-001, CH-005).** Die-cast, heavy in the hand, takes chrome, nickel, matte black and satin gold plating well. The default for mid-market kitchens.
- **Aluminium (CH-002).** Lighter and cheaper per piece, best anodised or powder coated in matte black. Bare polished aluminium dulls quickly in humid air.
- **Stainless steel 304 (CH-003).** The right answer for bathrooms and coastal installations where rust complaints are expensive.
- **Brass (CH-004).** Antique brass and lacquered brass finish for premium joinery; unlacquered brass tarnishes, which some buyers want and most do not.
- **Plastic + metal (CH-006).** Budget concealed knob for rental units and budget wardrobes. Sell it as a price-entry line, never as a durable one.

Plating thickness is what separates a two-year handle from a six-month handle. We specify 8-12 micron nickel under chrome on all metal models, and we will happily send a plating report if your buyer asks.
