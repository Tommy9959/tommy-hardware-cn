### What actually sells in the Nigerian furniture market

Nigerian furniture production runs on particle board and MDF, and that dictates which fittings move:

- **Cam lock connectors (minifix).** Every knockdown wardrobe and bed frame in Nigeria uses them. A wholesaler typically moves 20,000-50,000 sets per container, split between 15 mm and 18 mm board sizes. Zinc alloy bodies with a steel bolt last far longer than all-plastic copies, and the price gap is only a few cents.
- **35 mm concealed hinges.** The 110° cup hinge with 3-way adjustment is the default. Straight-arm for full overlay doors, half-overlay and inset versions for older cabinets. Soft-close dampers are now expected in mid-market kitchen units sold in Lagos and Abuja.
- **Aluminum handles.** 96 mm, 128 mm and 160 mm hole centres cover almost every kitchen and wardrobe door. Matte black and brushed nickel outsell chrome in the newer estates; chrome still dominates the replacement market in the open markets.
- **Support legs and adjustable feet.** Cabinet legs with a steel insert do not collapse under a loaded kitchen unit. Plastic-only legs fail in the heat — we sell both so a wholesaler can offer a budget and a durable line.
- **Magnet catches, shelf pins, edge trims.** Low-value items, but they are what brings a carpenter back to the same wholesaler every week.

### Materials that survive humidity and coastal air

Lagos and Port Harcourt are coastal; Onitsha and Aba are humid most of the year. Cheap painted steel fittings rust inside a season, and that is the number one complaint end users bring back to the wholesaler.

- **Zinc alloy + nickel plating** for cams, handles and hinges — plating thickness matters more than the alloy name.
- **Cold rolled steel with 8-12 micron nickel** for concealed hinges; below that, rust starts at the hinge cup.
- **Stainless steel 304** where the budget allows, especially for bathroom and outdoor furniture.
- **Avoid** unplated black-painted iron for anything installed near a window or in a bathroom.
