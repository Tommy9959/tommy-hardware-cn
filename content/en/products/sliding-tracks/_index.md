### What to stock for the Nigerian market

- **400 mm and 450 mm full extension slides (ST-001).** These fit the kitchen and wardrobe drawer boxes that dominate local production. 25-35 kg rating is enough for domestic use; quote the heavier slide only where the customer stores tools or pots.
- **Soft-close slides (ST-004).** Now standard in mid-market kitchens in Lekki, Ikoyi and Abuja. The damper is the failure point — insist on a branded damper and test 20 cycles before loading.
- **Wardrobe rails (ST-002).** Anodised aluminium bottom and top rails, usually sold with nylon rollers. Match the rail profile to the roller you stock or the whole system fails at installation.
- **Heavy duty track (ST-003).** Workshop doors, sliding room dividers and shop fronts. 50 kg/m rating with nylon rollers that will not flat-spot.
- **Glass door track (ST-005).** Stainless with brass rollers for shop display units and bathroom partitions.
- **Barn door hardware (ST-006).** Growing fast in Lagos residential fit-outs. Matte black powder coated steel sells; rustic finishes are slower.

### Length, load and finish: the three specifications that matter

1. **Length.** Slides are sold by the pair in specific lengths; rails by the metre. Mixing these two units on one invoice is the most common costing error we see with new importers.
2. **Load rating.** A 35 kg slide on a 600 mm drawer holding pots will fail. Ask for the cycle-tested rating, not the catalogue maximum.
3. **Finish.** Zinc plating thickness of 8-12 micron is the minimum for coastal Nigeria. Bare or thinly plated steel seizes and stains the drawer within months, and the return cost is far higher than the plating difference.
