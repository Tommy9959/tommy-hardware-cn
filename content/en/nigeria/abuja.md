## The short version

Abuja does not buy the way Onitsha buys. There is no carton-by-carton market rhythm here; there are **estates, government buildings and hotels**, and they buy against a door schedule with a specification attached.

That changes what a supplier has to do: match finishes, supply the whole door package, and be able to answer a consultant's question about grade.

## What Abuja projects specify

- **SS304 lever handles** in satin or polished finish, matched across the building.
- **Mortise lock sets** rather than loose locks, so the hardware arrives as a unit.
- **Concealed or SS304 butt hinges**, depending on the door type.
- **Door closers** on corridor and fire doors.
- **Accessories as a set** — stoppers, viewers, numerals — in the same finish family.

Finish consistency is the detail that decides whether a project looks finished or assembled. It is also the reason a project order is quoted as a package, not line by line.

## Transit and clearing

Abuja is inland: containers clear at **Lagos or Onne** and move up by truck, **1-2 days from Lagos**. Two implications:

1. **Clearing time dominates.** The sea leg is predictable; the port is not. Build slack into the programme.
2. **Deliveries arrive in phases.** Most estates take the hardware in two or three drops rather than one, so carton labelling by block or floor saves site time.

Documentation is unchanged: Form M before shipment, SONCAP certificate, PAAR at the port.

## FAQ

**Can you quote against a door schedule?** Yes — send the schedule with door types and quantities, and the quote comes back as a package with carton counts.

**Are samples available for approval?** Yes, samples can be couriered before the order is placed, which is standard for estate work.
