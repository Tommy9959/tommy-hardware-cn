Door Lock Price in Nigeria 2026 | Best Selling Locks with Real Prices

📅 April 29, 2026 ✍️ Tommy — JH Hardware (10 Years in Nigeria) 🏷️ door locks , what sells , Nigeria market , sales data , from our experience

What Door Locks Actually Sell in Nigeria (10 Years of Sales Data)

Let me show you something — here’s our actual sales breakdown from 2025:

Lock Type Percentage of Sales
Mortise Locks 45%
Padlocks 35%
Cylinder Locks 10%
Smart Locks 6%
Everything Else 4%

That’s right — mortise locks and padlocks make up 80% of our sales. Smart locks are growing, but they’re still a tiny part of the market.

This guide isn’t about “the best locks” — it’s about what actually sells in Nigeria, based on 10 years of orders from Lagos, Port Harcourt, Abuja, and beyond.

Mortise Locks — The 45% of Our Sales That Pays the Bills

Mortise locks are our bread and butter. Every builder, every hardware shop, every estate — they all buy mortise locks.

What Nigerians Actually Buy

Most Popular: $3.00 - $6.00 Mortise Lock

This is the sweet spot. It’s not the cheapest, but it’s not the most expensive either. It feels solid, works for 10-15 years, and customers trust it.

Who Buys This:

  • Residential builders — our #1 customer
  • Estate developers — putting 10-20 locks per building
  • Hardware shops in every city — this is their top seller

What They Order:

  • Single cylinder (key outside, thumb turn inside) — 85% of orders
  • Satin nickel finish — 60% of orders
  • 50-100 per order for builders, 200-500 for big distributors

Quick Story

In 2021, an Abuja developer ordered 2,000 mortise locks from us. He chose the $4.20 model (not the cheapest, not the most expensive). He’s ordered from us every year since — last month he bought 3,000.

Why? He told me “my customers don’t complain about these locks, and they don’t come back asking for replacements.”

What Doesn’t Sell (As Much)

  • Super cheap locks ($2.00 or less) — customers say they feel “flimsy”
  • Double cylinder — only 15% of orders (safety concern in emergencies)
  • Fancy finishes — gold, antique bronze — they look nice but don’t sell

Padlocks — The 35% of Sales That Everyone Needs

Padlocks are universal. Everyone buys padlocks — shop owners, homeowners, market traders, warehouse managers.

What Actually Sells

#1 Seller: $1.00 - $3.00 Standard Padlock (50mm)

This is our top-selling item overall. Why? Because everyone needs one. It’s not high security, but it’s cheap, it works, and people replace them often.

#2 Seller: $2.00 - $5.00 Stainless Steel Padlock

Big seller in Lagos and Port Harcourt — coastal cities, so rust is a problem. Customers pay a bit more for stainless steel because it lasts.

#3 Seller: $5.00 - $12.00 Heavy-Duty Padlock

For shops, warehouses, and people who need more security. Smaller volume, but higher margins.

Quick Story

A Kano distributor orders 5,000 standard padlocks every 3 months. He told me “people lose keys, padlocks get cut, they rust — I always sell out of padlocks.”

Padlocks are the gift that keeps on giving — because they get replaced often.

Smart Locks — The 6% That’s Growing Fast (But Not for Everyone)

Smart locks are the future — but the future isn’t here yet for most of Nigeria.

What’s Selling (A Little Bit)

Fingerprint Smart Locks ($30 - $60)

This is the only smart lock that sells consistently for us. Who buys it?

  • Luxury estates in Lagos (Ikoyi, Victoria Island)
  • Abuja government buildings
  • Tech-savvy homeowners in the big cities

What They Like:

  • No keys to lose
  • Fingerprint access — convenient
  • Modern “premium” look

What’s Holding It Back

  1. Cost — $30+ is still too expensive for most Nigerians
  2. Power — battery life is OK, but people worry about dead batteries
  3. Familiarity — people still prefer physical keys
  4. Repairs — if it breaks, not many people can fix it

Quick Story

In 2023, a Lagos developer tried 100 fingerprint locks for a new estate. He ended up ordering only 50 the next year. Why? “They look great, but when the battery dies, tenants get angry.”

Smart locks are growing, but they’re still a niche product.

Cylinder Locks — The 10% of Sales That Keeps Coming Back

Cylinder locks are the “replacement” market. When a customer’s existing lock breaks, they usually just replace the cylinder, not the entire lock.

What Sells

$1.00 - $5.00 Euro Profile Cylinder

This is our top seller in this category. Builders and hardware shops like it because it’s easy to replace.

Who Buys:

  • Maintenance teams for estates
  • Hardware shops — people come in needing a replacement
  • DIY homeowners

Climate Matters — What Sells Where

Lagos & Port Harcourt (Coastal, Humid)

Top Sellers:

  1. Mortise locks with stainless steel components
  2. Stainless steel padlocks
  3. Regular mortise locks with anti-corrosion finish

What Doesn’t Sell:

  • Iron/steel padlocks without coating (they rust too fast)
  • Cheap locks — customers want something that lasts in the humidity

Abuja, Kano (Inland, Dry)

Top Sellers:

  1. Standard mortise locks (SS201 works great here)
  2. Standard padlocks (rust isn’t a big problem)
  3. Smart locks (Abuja has more buyers for premium products)

What Works Here:

  • SS201 is fine for inland — no salt air to worry about
  • Budget options sell well

Our Recommendation (What We Tell New Customers)

If You’re Just Starting

  1. Start with mortise locks — 45% of our sales, low risk
  2. Add padlocks — 35% of sales, everyone needs them
  3. Get a few smart lock samples — show them to premium customers
  4. Skip the fancy stuff — fancy finishes don’t sell (yet)

If You’re Established

  1. Mix it: 60% mid-range mortise locks, 30% padlocks, 10% other
  2. Listen to your customers: if they keep asking for smart locks, stock them
  3. Keep a few stainless steel padlocks for coastal customers
