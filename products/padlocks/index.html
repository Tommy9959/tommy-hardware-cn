What actually sells in Nigeria

  • 50-60mm chrome square and rectangular iron (SF, SFN, HLV) — the volume sellers. Shop doors, house gates, school lockers; sold by the dozen in every building-materials market.
  • 45-80mm waterproof plastic cover (GTK) — the coastal answer. The cover keeps rain off the cylinder, which is where cheap padlocks seize.
  • 74-94mm heavy duty (HHL) — warehouses, containers, trucks and factory yards. 12.8mm shackle, roughly 1kg of lock.
  • Keyed alike (YGTK) — one key for a whole building: schools, hotels, estates, multi-unit properties.

Shackle and cylinder: what to look at before you buy

  • Shackle diameter decides what a bolt cutter does to it. 50mm locks typically use 8mm, 70mm uses 10mm, and the heavy 74-94mm range goes to 12.8mm.
  • Cylinder type decides how long the lock keeps working. Solid brass cylinders resist seizing; iron disc cylinders are cheaper and fine for dry inland markets.
  • Body finish is what the customer sees first: chrome, golden, nickel, or imitate-brass. Match the finish to what your market already stocks rather than trying to change buying habits.

Climate guidance for Nigerian buyers

  • Coastal (Lagos, Port Harcourt, Calabar, Warri): specify the plastic-cover GTK or YGTK. Salt air and two rainy seasons will find any unprotected cylinder.
  • Inland (Kano, Kaduna, Abuja, Jos): chrome and golden square iron models hold up well and keep your price point low.
